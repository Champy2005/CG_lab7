3    The fragment shader as a function

A fragment shader is a function from one pixel to one color. It gets gl_FragCoord, the pixel
center, and the uniform block. It cannot loop over the image, it does not know what its neighbors
are doing, and it runs half a million times per frame. Everything in Parts I to III is that one
function written better.


Two coordinates.      uv is the pixel as a fraction of the target, 0 to 1 across and down: right
for sampling, wrong for shapes, because the target is not square. Divide by the height in both
components instead and you get a centered, aspect-corrected p:

    One number instead of a shape.            A signed distance function returns the distance from
    p to the nearest surface, negative inside, zero on it, positive outside (7.3.3, DEF-7.02).
    length(p) - 0.5 is a disc; two of them combine with min. No mesh, no point-in-polygon test,
    and the same eld gives you the outline, the ll, the glow and the antialiasing.


    The edge.      A shape cut with d   < 0.0 has stair steps on its rim. smoothstep ramps across the
    rim instead (EQ-7.02):

                                                                                        
                                               2                        x − e0
                    smoothstep(e0 , e1 , x) = t (3 − 2t),     t = clamp         , 0, 1                (2)
                                                                        e1 − e0
    With e0 > e1 the ramp runs backwards, which is what you want when the inside of the shape is
    the negative side: smoothstep(0.02,     -0.02, d) is 0 well outside and 1 well inside. But 0.02 is
    a distance in p units, and a p unit is 300 pixels here and 1080 on a 4K screen. Your antialiasing
    should be one pixel wide, not one number you picked.


    fwidth, and why a lone pixel can hold a derivative. fwidth(d) is |∂d/∂x| + |∂d/∂y|:
    how much d changes between this pixel and the next.      Substitute it for the constant and
    smoothstep(w, -w, d) is one pixel wide at every resolution.
         How does a shader that only knows its own pixel produce a derivative? It does not: the GPU
    shades pixels in quads, blocks of 2 × 2, and a derivative is the dierence between neighbors in the
    same quad. Same hardware fact as Lab 06 Part III, where you measured a subgroup by making
    neighboring pixels branch dierently. Two consequences: a quad straddling the edge of a triangle
    runs fragments that are thrown away afterwards, purely so their neighbors have something to
    dierentiate against, and fwidth inside an if that not all four pixels take is undened.


    Debug by color.       There is no printf in a fragment shader, so you output the number you
    want to see as a color (7.1.5, F-7.01). Every shader here has a u.mode switch: 0 is the picture,
    1 to 3 show a variable. Most of those views are given. You will nd out what they are for the
    rst time something is wrong.


    Why the hash is integer arithmetic.            Every noise tutorial, your chapter's 7.3.2 included,
    starts with fract(sin(dot(p,      ...)) * 43758.5453). It works, and it is not reproducible: sin
    of a large argument is where GPU vendors disagree most, so the same shader draws a dierent
    terrain on the machine next to you and no reference image can ever match.         noise.glsl gives
    you a PCG integer hash instead. Integer multiply, shift and xor are exact and identical on every
    GPU and on the CPU, which is why the panel can compute the same number in C++ and hold
    your shader to it.

                     0.052 away : after a smooth union d is a bound , not a distance

Nothing is within 0.010 of that pixel. The nearest surface, the corner of the rounded box, is 0.052
away.   op_smooth_union subtracts k h(1 − h), which pulls the eld down near the junction and
invents a surface neither primitive has. The sign is still right everywhere and the value is still
never larger than the true distance, which is all the rendering needs, but it has stopped being a
distance. In a ray marcher that is the dierence between stepping safely and stepping through
the wall.
